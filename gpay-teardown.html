1. Executive Summary & Architectural Security Layers

India’s Unified Payments Interface (UPI) processes over 13,000 crore annual transactions. Its explosive adoption relies on sub-3-second real-time push payments. However, because settlement is instantaneous and irreversible, fraudsters have shifted away from technical breaches toward social engineering—psychologically manipulating victims via phone calls to authorize payments themselves.

This study combines an architectural security teardown of UPI with a comprehensive Product Requirements Document (PRD) for Pay-Pause: an intelligent, risk-scored pre-PIN intervention for Google Pay.

UPI's Multi-Tiered Security Model

Layer 1: Device Binding

"Something You Have": Outbound encrypted SMS cryptographically links the physical SIM card (ICCID/IMSI) to the registered bank account. Completely eliminates web-based credential hijacking.

Layer 2: NPCI Common Library

"Something You Know": Google Pay never sees or logs the UPI PIN. The PIN pad is an isolated secure sandbox rendered by NPCI, encrypted with the remitter bank's public key.

Layer 3: Risk Scoring

Behavioral Telemetry: Sub-second ML models detect screen-sharing apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer), unusual velocity spikes, and high-risk beneficiary VPAs before routing.

2. The Core Paradox: Social Engineering & Failure Points

Despite military-grade cryptography and device binding, traditional cybersecurity collapses when a user is manipulated into entering their own PIN under psychological distress:

  • The Coercion Vector: Fraudsters systematically manufacture panic over live phone calls (fake police digital arrests, courier parcel drugs, immediate electricity cutoffs, or customer care refunds).
  • The Speed Trap: In genuine payments, zero friction is an asset. Under phone coercion, zero friction is a liability because victims rush through PIN entry before their rational faculties can intervene.
  • The Fraud Scale (RBI Data): Reported UPI fraud rose to 13.42 lakh incidents (₹1,087 crore) in FY2023–24. High-value transactions above ₹10,000 account for approximately 98.5% of total fraud value.

3. Market Gap Analysis & The Risk Triad

Existing safety interventions either fire on narrow hardware signals or propose clumsy blanket delays that damage UPI's core value proposition:

Protection LayerOperating MechanismCritical Gaps & Limitations
Android In-Call Protection Detects financial apps opened during active screen sharing on calls; offers 30s pause. Requires active screen share; completely blind to voice-only psychological coercion calls.
Pixel Call Scam Detection On-device Gemini Nano analyzes voice audio in Hindi/English for scam phrases. Restricted to Pixel 9+ devices (<1% of India’s active smartphone base).
Google Pay Security Alerts Delivers dynamic warning banners for unverified accounts (>1M warnings weekly). High false-positive rate causes alert fatigue; lacks live phone call context integration.
RBI April 2026 Paper Proposal Proposes mandatory 1-hour delay for all first-time transfers >₹10,000. Blanket delays destroy UPI’s real-time payment utility. Industry demands dynamic risk scoring.
Pay-Pause (Our Proposal) Evaluates the combined Risk Triad on-device before initializing the NPCI PIN pad. Fires only when multiple risk signals converge; zero friction for standard payments.

The Unaddressed Gap: The Real-Time Risk Triad

No existing production system evaluates the convergence of these three signals in real time without recording audio:

[Active Voice Call with Unknown Number] + [First-Time P2P Payee] + [High Transaction Value ≥ ₹10,000]

4. Proposed Solution: The Pay-Pause Architecture & Tiered UX

Pay-Pause runs client-side on the Pre-PIN Payment Review Screen (after tapping Proceed to Pay, immediately before invoking the NPCI Common Library SDK):

Risk LevelTrigger CriteriaUX Intervention & Friction Design
Tier 0: Low Risk Known payee in contact book OR no active call OR transaction value < ₹2,000. Standard Flow: Zero added friction. Proceeds instantly to the NPCI PIN pad.
Tier 1: Medium Risk First-time payee + amount ≥ ₹10,000 OR active unknown call + amount ₹2,000–₹9,999. Inline Warning Banner: Amber banner stating "On a call with an unknown number? Scammers often rush payments." User taps "I trust this payee" to proceed.
Tier 2: High Risk Triad Alignment: Active unknown call + First-time P2P payee + Amount ≥ ₹10,000. 15-Second Pattern-Breaking Modal: Full-screen bottom sheet with a 15-second countdown timer ring.
• Headline: "Stop and check. Banks, police, and customer support will NEVER ask you to send money over a phone call."
• Primary Action (Red CTA): End Call & Cancel Payment.
• Secondary Action (Enabled after 15s): Proceed to PIN or Call Trusted Contact.

REQ-1P0On-Device Call State SignalDetects active cellular/VoIP call and checks local contact book matching strictly on-device without audio recording permissions. Latency must be < 100ms (p99).
REQ-2P0NPCI Common Library GateFor Tier 2 events, the application prevents invocation of the NPCI PIN SDK until the 15-second timer expires and explicit confirmation is made.
REQ-3P0One-Tap Call TerminationTapping "End Call & Cancel Payment" cancels transaction intent and drops the active phone call immediately.
REQ-4P0Permission Degradation FallbackIf contact/phone permissions are withheld, the engine falls back to evaluating payee novelty + rapid clipboard paste + transaction value.
REQ-5P1Post-Call Cooldown WindowIf an unknown call ends, Tier 2 protection remains active for 3 minutes post-hangup to prevent scammers from instructing victims to "hang up and pay quickly."
REQ-6P1Multilingual Voice Read-OutTier 2 modals feature an audio icon reading warnings in the active app language (Hindi, English, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali).
REQ-7P1Verified Merchant (P2M) ExemptionPayments to verified merchant QR codes or registered payment gateways bypass Tier 2 unless flagged for mule activity.

7. Privacy, Socioeconomic Fairness & Accessibility

  • Privacy-by-Design: Contact matching and call-state telemetry execute 100% on-device. Google servers receive only an anonymized binary trigger flag (is_unknown_call = true/false). Contact books, phone numbers, and audio are never transmitted off-device.
  • Socioeconomic Fairness: High-frequency P2P transactors (such as local traders and gig workers) receive dynamic risk adjustments after initial successful transactions to prevent recurring friction.
  • Accessibility (WCAG AA): Plain-language copy avoiding financial legalese (e.g., using "fake caller" instead of "social engineering attack"), auto-focused TalkBack screen reader support, and one-tap voice read-outs for low-literacy users.

8. Experimentation Plan & Strategic Decision Tree

We propose a randomized, stratified 2% A/B experiment on Android Google Pay in India across metro, Tier-2, and rural user demographics:

  • Phase 0 (Shadow Logging — 7 Days): Run signal detection silently in the background to verify trigger sparsity (<0.8% of user sessions) and confirm app performance stability.
  • Phase 1 (Active A/B Test — 4 Weeks): 1% Control (existing warnings) vs 1% Treatment (Pay-Pause Tier 1 & Tier 2).
  • Phase 2 (Observation — 14 Days): Post-test window to capture delayed bank chargebacks and cybercrime portal filings.

Strategic Rollout Decision Tree

  1. Was Fraud Value reduced by ≥ 20%?
    • NO: Rollback to Phase 0 and re-evaluate heuristic weights.
    • YES: Proceed to Guardrail Check.
  2. Is Genuine Payment Drop-off ≤ 1.5% and Cross-App Churn ≤ 3%?
    • YES: Greenlight — Scale rollout to 10% and prepare pan-India general availability.
    • NO: Iterate Thresholds — Raise amount threshold to ₹25,000 or shorten modal lockout from 15s to 10s.

9. Key PM Lessons as an Aspiring APM

Friction as a Feature

In instant push payments, zero friction is an asset for genuine users but a catastrophic vulnerability during live phone manipulation. Thoughtful friction saves lives and livelihoods.

Precision Over Blanket Mandates

While regulators propose blunt 1-hour blanket delays, rigorous product managers identify the specific risk triad to protect vulnerable users while preserving UPI's speed.

Adversarial Design

Good product design anticipates user needs; exceptional security product design anticipates the attacker's counter-strategy (e.g., cooldown windows against scammer coaching).
